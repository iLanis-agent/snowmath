Snow math

A snowfall goes wrong in three places: the "light dusting" that turns out to weigh half a ton on the drive, the clear-the-drive plan that was never clocked against an honest shoveling rate, and the ice melt bought by vibes instead of by area. Snow math turns the forecast into the kilos, turns the kilos into the minutes, and turns the drive into the bags. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Load plan - the area and the snowfall at a labeled density become the cubic meters and the kilos.
  • Time plan - the kilos at a labeled shoveling rate become the minutes on the shovel.
  • Melt plan - the area at a labeled dose becomes the kilos and the bags of ice melt.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: 100 kg per m3 of fresh snow, a 15 kg-per-minute shoveling rate, 100 g of ice melt per m2 and 10 kg bags - all illustrative defaults you can change by measuring. Snow gets heavier as it wets and ages, and shoveling is real exertion: pace yourself, take breaks, and hire it out if your doctor has ever mentioned your heart. This is planning arithmetic, not a weather service.
